# Best Mystery Books 2025: Cozy Mysteries, Crime Fiction and Whodunits

There is a particular pleasure to mystery reading that no other genre quite replicates. The combination of a puzzle to solve, a world to inhabit, and characters to follow across multiple books creates a distinctive reading experience that, at its best, is endlessly renewable. 2025 has been an exceptional year for mystery fiction across all its many subgenres.

## The Subgenres of Mystery Fiction

Before diving into recommendations, it helps to understand the landscape.

**Cozy mysteries** feature amateur sleuths in small-town or village settings, minimal graphic violence, a strong sense of community, and often a professional theme (baking, bookshops, cats). They prioritize charm and puzzle over darkness.

**Hard-boiled detective fiction** features morally complex private investigators in urban settings. The writing is typically terse and atmospheric. Think Philip Marlowe, Sam Spade, or their modern descendants.

**Police procedural** follows law enforcement through the technical and human process of investigation. The emphasis is on realistic detection rather than heroic individual brilliance.

**Cozy historical mystery** combines the charm of the cozy with a historical period setting. Enormously popular and consistently entertaining.

**Literary crime fiction** uses the machinery of crime and investigation to explore character, society, and moral complexity at a literary level.

## Best Cozy Mystery of 2025

**The Thursday Murder Club series by Richard Osman** continues to be the gold standard of modern cozy mysteries. Four retirement community residents with improbable investigative skills solve crimes the police cannot crack. Osman's combination of genuine wit, warm characterization, and satisfying plotting has made this one of the most beloved mystery series of recent years. Start with The Thursday Murder Club and proceed in order.

**The Marlow Murder Club by Robert Thorogood** offers a similar appeal: a woman who has moved to the English town of Marlow to live quietly ends up at the center of a murder investigation. Perfect cozy atmosphere with more genuine psychological depth than the genre usually delivers.

**Arsenic and Adobo by Mia P. Manansala** launches a Filipino American cozy series set in a small-town restaurant. The food descriptions alone justify reading, and the mystery plotting is tightly constructed.

## Best Hard-Boiled Mystery of 2025

**The Night Circus by Erin Morgenstern** is not strictly hard-boiled but shares the genre's atmospheric intensity in a lush fantasy setting, and mystery readers who want something distinctive should consider it.

**A Beautiful Crime by Christopher Bollen** follows two young men in New York who travel to Venice to execute an elaborate art theft. The literary quality is exceptional and the Venice atmosphere is intoxicating.

**The New York Detective by William Marshall** brings a classic detective back in a richly imagined period recreation of 1880s New York.

## Best Police Procedural of 2025

**The Long Sunset by John Sandford** continues his Virgil Flowers series with the reliability and skill that has made Sandford one of the most consistently excellent crime fiction writers of his generation.

**Still Life by Louise Penny** begins the Inspector Gamache series, one of the finest mystery series of the 21st century, set in the fictional Quebec village of Three Pines. If you have not started this series, 2025 is the year. The characterization, writing quality, and atmospheric depth are extraordinary.

**The Dry by Jane Harper** is an Australian procedural that became an international sensation. A Federal Agent returns to his drought-stricken hometown to attend a funeral and becomes entangled in a decades-old mystery. Atmospheric, tense, and beautifully written.

## Best Historical Mystery of 2025

**The Locked Door by Freida McFadden** combines a historical mystery with a contemporary thriller in a dual-timeline structure that delivers genuine surprises.

**The Maid by Nita Prose** launched one of the most beloved recent mystery series with its unforgettable protagonist Molly the Maid, a woman with an unusual literal-minded perception of the world who becomes the prime suspect in a hotel murder. Charming, original, and genuinely moving.

**The Thursday Murder Club** technically qualifies here too, given its retirement community setting and the age of its protagonists who carry decades of historical context into every case.

## Best International Mystery Fiction

Some of the finest mystery writing comes from outside the English-speaking world.

**The Hunters by Tom Rob Smith** explores Cold War espionage with thriller momentum and literary ambition that transcends genre boundaries.

**Jar City by Arnaldur Indridason** introduces Icelandic detective Erlendur and remains one of the finest introductions to Nordic noir in any language.

**The Devotion of Suspect X by Keigo Higashino** is the most celebrated Japanese mystery novel of the modern era. A brilliant mathematician constructs an elaborate alibi for a crime he witnessed. The novel's structure, revealing who committed the crime from the start and then following the investigation, creates a completely original kind of suspense.

## Building a Mystery Reading Life

The mystery genre rewards series reading more than any other. Finding a detective you love and following them through 10 or 20 books delivers a reading experience that is deeply satisfying in a way single novels cannot match.

Use library apps like Libby to test series before buying. Read new releases from authors you trust. Follow mystery-focused book clubs like The Poisoned Pen or crime fiction review outlets.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best classic mystery series to read?**
Agatha Christie's Hercule Poirot series remains essential. Start with Murder on the Orient Express or And Then There Were None. Dorothy L. Sayers' Lord Peter Wimsey series is equally classic and even more literate. Arthur Conan Doyle's Sherlock Holmes stories are the foundation of the entire genre.

**Are cozy mysteries worth reading if I usually read literary fiction?**
The best cozy mysteries, like those by Richard Osman or Louise Penny, have genuine literary qualities: strong characterization, meaningful themes, and distinctive prose voices. They are pleasurable without being shallow.

**How do I find new mystery authors I will love?**
If you love one author, research who they cite as influences and who has won the same awards they have won. The Edgar Award and CWA Gold Dagger are the genre's most reliable indicators of quality.

## Conclusion

Mystery fiction in 2025 offers an embarrassment of riches: clever puzzles, richly atmospheric settings, detective characters you will want to follow for decades, and writing that increasingly refuses to choose between genre pleasure and literary quality. Pick one book from the list above and you will almost certainly find yourself wanting more.
